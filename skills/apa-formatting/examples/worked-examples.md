# Worked APA examples

**All people, publications, institutions, dates, quotations, URLs, and source
metadata in these examples are FICTIONAL. They illustrate formatting only.**
The example.com URLs are deliberately illustrative and are not evidence.

## 1. Two-author journal article: metadata to citation pair

Input: Ana Vega and Basil Noor; 2024; article “Reading beside the river”; journal
“Journal of Imaginary Learning”; volume 8; issue 2; pages 14–27; no DOI; public
article URL https://example.com/fictional-reading.

Reference:

Vega, A., & Noor, B. (2024). Reading beside the river. *Journal of Imaginary Learning, 8*(2), 14–27. https://example.com/fictional-reading

Parenthetical paraphrase: Frequent reading was associated with sustained attention
(Vega & Noor, 2024).

Narrative: Vega and Noor (2024) reported an association with sustained attention.

Audit judgment: Correct formatting would not establish that this association is
true. With a real supplied article, read the relevant passage before endorsing it.

## 2. Quote boundary and locators

Short quote:

Noor (2024) wrote, “Readers returned to the same passage” (p. 18).

The following invented passage is exactly **40 words**. In a word processor,
apply a 0.5-inch block indent, double spacing, and no enclosing quotation marks.
This Markdown blockquote only marks the content to receive that treatment:

> Readers described the quiet room as a place where they could return to difficult
> ideas without interruption. They did not agree on one ideal schedule, but each
> valued having enough time to reconsider a passage and discuss it with others.
> (Noor, 2024, p. 18)

For an unpaginated fictional webpage use a verified locator such as
`(Coastal Learning Institute, 2024, Study Rooms section, para. 2)`, never a guessed
print page.

## 3. Absent date, group author, and matching reference

Verified fictional input: Organization author Coastal Learning Institute; title
“Planning a reading group”; no publication/update date anywhere on the work;
same organization is site name; URL https://example.com/fictional-group.

Coastal Learning Institute. (n.d.). *Planning a reading group*. https://example.com/fictional-group

First citation: `(Coastal Learning Institute [CLI], n.d.)`; later `(CLI, n.d.)`.
If the date was merely not supplied, the correct response would request/check it
and mark the draft unresolved instead of asserting that the work has no date.

## 4. Book and same-year ordering

Noor, B. (2024a). *Attention in quiet places* (2nd ed.). Imaginary Press.

Noor, B. (2024b). *Reading after sunset*. Imaginary Press.

Use `(Noor, 2024a)` for the first title even if *Reading after sunset* was the
first book cited in the paper. Alphabetical reference order determines letters.

## 5. Secondary evidence and private communication

Input: You read Vega's 2024 book describing Noor's unavailable 1998 study.
Write `(Noor, 1998, as cited in Vega, 2024)`; include only Vega's consulted book
in references. Do not create a reference for Noor's unread study.

Input: Basil Noor privately emailed you on March 2, 2024, and readers cannot
retrieve the exchange. Write `(B. Noor, personal communication, March 2, 2024)`
in text only; do not add an email reference.
