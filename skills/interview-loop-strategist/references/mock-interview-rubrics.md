# Candidate-Controlled Practice Rubrics

Use this reference to create a practice rubric that makes observable work useful without treating a mock session as a hiring signal. Select only the sections that fit the candidate's stated goal and the round actually being rehearsed.

## Practice contract

Before scoring, record the candidate's goal, chosen prompt, allowed materials, modality, timebox, accessibility needs, and which artifacts they consent to retain. A missing recording, transcript, or whiteboard is a feedback limit, not a performance finding.

## Evidence scale

For each selected dimension, use one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning | Required note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed | A retained artifact directly shows the behavior. | Cite the timestamp, code location, or candidate-provided note. |
| Partial | The artifact suggests the behavior but leaves material gaps. | State the gap and a low-risk next experiment. |
| Unavailable | The session did not produce enough relevant evidence. | Do not infer a score or trait; offer an optional way to practice it. |

Do not convert labels into a total score, percentile, pass/fail result, readiness rating, or hiring prediction.

## Reusable dimensions

### Problem framing

| Dimension | Look for | Candidate-facing experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constraints | The candidate states functional, scale, correctness, or accessibility constraints before a solution. | Start the next answer with two constraints and invite a clarification. |
| Assumptions | The candidate labels unknowns and asks targeted questions. | Write down one assumption before selecting a design. |
| Tradeoffs | The candidate compares alternatives against a stated constraint. | Name one rejected option and the reason it did not fit. |

### Technical reasoning

| Dimension | Look for | Candidate-facing experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | The response progresses through a legible sequence rather than an unconnected list. | Use a short outline before implementation or diagramming. |
| Validation | The candidate describes tests, failure modes, or checks relevant to the chosen solution. | Reserve two minutes to test an edge case or name a recovery path. |
| Adaptation | The candidate revises the approach when a new requirement appears and explains why. | Practice one follow-up constraint after the initial solution. |

### Story and collaboration reasoning

| Dimension | Look for | Candidate-facing experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | The candidate distinguishes their own decisions from team or organizational context. | Mark "I", "we", and unknown details in a draft story. |
| Specificity | The response connects actions to candidate-provided evidence or labels details to verify. | Replace one broad claim with a concrete, truthful example. |
| Reflection | The candidate explains a learning or changed approach without inventing a positive outcome. | Add one honest "what I would do differently" sentence. |

## Debrief format

For each finding, produce:

1. **Observation:** a link to a consented artifact.
2. **Rubric dimension:** the specific item above or a candidate-defined one.
3. **Confidence:** observed, partial, or unavailable, with the reason.
4. **Experiment:** one candidate-optional change for the next practice session.

End with no more than three experiments. Ask the candidate whether to keep, revise, or discard each one.
