# Target-process research for interview practice

Use this guide only when the candidate chooses an organization or role to research. No company, interview sequence, difficulty, values framework, compensation, or preparation workload is assumed.

## Establish the research scope

Record the selected role, interview stage, known dates, and questions the candidate wants answered. If no target is selected, retain a general software-engineering practice plan.

Ask for candidate-supplied recruiter guidance or a process description if available. Do not solicit confidential interview questions or infer employer requirements from a sparse candidate profile.

## Build a dated source record

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Question | Round format, permitted materials, duration, accessibility process, or another candidate-selected concern. |
| Evidence | Candidate-supplied correspondence or a direct link to an official recruiting source. |
| Date and scope | Record when it was supplied or retrieved and the role, location, and stage it covers. |
| Status | Confirmed for this candidate, public general guidance, tentative inference, conflicting, or unknown. |
| Open question | The specific detail still needing confirmation. |

Candidate-specific recruiter instructions can differ from general public guidance. Preserve both sources and their scopes; ask the candidate which process applies instead of silently resolving a conflict.

Public anecdotes may suggest questions to ask but do not establish the candidate's loop. Never describe an undated template as the current process.

## Convert process evidence into optional practice

1. Show confirmed rounds separately from tentative coverage.
2. Offer relevant specialist practice using the main skill's routing table.
3. Show the time cost and what each proposed block would displace.
4. Let the candidate accept, edit, or decline each change.
5. Record the request, source, rationale, and review point in the adjustment log.

Do not allocate time from company prestige, supposed difficulty rankings, inferred values alignment, or an assumed hiring bar. A company name does not establish readiness or hiring likelihood.

## Example

The candidate supplies a recruiter email confirming one coding discussion and one project deep dive but no durations. Propose one coding rehearsal and one discussion of a project the candidate selects. Keep durations unknown until confirmed; ask before changing their existing eight-hour weekly practice budget.

## Boundaries

This guide supports format research and candidate-owned preparation. It does not score people, predict pass/fail or offers, compare candidates, manufacture stories, or promise negotiation outcomes.
