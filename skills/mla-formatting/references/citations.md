# Citations and Works Cited

Use when creating or repairing MLA citations. Evidence checked 2026-10-25;
[references/sources.md](sources.md) records direct official sources and limits.

## In-text keys

Use the shortest unambiguous entry key plus a supplied locator for a specific passage.
Ordinary author-page form is `(Mora 18)`, without year, comma, or `p.`. If Mora is already named
in prose, use `(18)`. With two authors use `(Mora and Vale 18)`; with three or more use
`(Mora et al. 18)`. Do not change the source's author order.

For several works under the same author key, include a short title:
`(Mora, Harbor Notes 18)` with the book title italicized, or `(Mora, “Night Walks” 18)`.
Distinguish identical surnames with initials or fuller names as necessary. Separate multiple cited
works with a semicolon, making each independent key clear. If uncertain about a crowded parenthetical,
name works in prose instead.

No author: first verify whether an organization is responsible; otherwise begin the entry with its
title and cite a short title, keeping the title's quotation marks or italics. Do not invent Anonymous.
If an organization is both author and publisher, the official no-author guidance puts it in the
publisher slot and starts with the title; the in-text key must follow the actual entry.
No page/part numbers: omit the locator, rather than counting browser paragraphs or using screen numbers.
If the author or title is clear in prose and no locator exists, no redundant parentheses are needed.
For time-based media, use an available timestamp for the relevant passage.

Indirect quotation: seek the original first. When only a secondary source was consulted, identify
the original speaker in prose and use `qtd. in` plus the consulted source key/locator, for example
`(qtd. in Vale 42)`. List the source actually consulted; do not imply you read the original.
The accessible official indirect-source example is labeled MLA 8; verify unusual applications
against MLA 9 section 6.77.

## Construct entries from facts, not a rigid source-type recipe

The [current practice template](https://style.mla.org/interactive-practice-template/) gives this order:

1. Author.
2. Title of source.
3. Title of container,
4. Other contributors,
5. Version,
6. Number,
7. Publisher,
8. Publication date,
9. Location.

Use only applicable, verified elements; adjust punctuation when elements are absent. Names normally
start surname, given name; for two authors reverse only the first and connect the second with `and`.
For three or more list the first followed by `et al.`. Italicize independent work/container titles;
put article, chapter, and other contained-work titles in quotation marks. Preserve meaningful title
styling and source language. MLA English titles generally use title capitalization; do not blindly
rewrite quoted titles in other languages or video titles copied from the platform.

A container is an enclosing work, such as a book containing a chapter or a journal containing an
article. A whole printed book needs no container. A database containing the journal can be a second
container: finish the first container with a period and repeat applicable elements 3–9 for the second.
A browser, search engine, or incidental link is not automatically a container. Select facts for the
version actually used, not every possible host. Do not invent volume, issue, editor, publisher, or date.
Journal entries normally omit the journal's publisher; web publisher omission can depend on redundancy.

For online locations prefer DOI, then permalink, then URL. Format a DOI as `https://doi.org/...`;
other URLs may usually omit the scheme. Include a journal article's supplied page span as well as
its online location. Prefer the work's direct stable link over a search-result URL.
An access date goes at the end, particularly for undated, changing, or removed pages; it is not a
replacement publication date and is not mandatory for every stable dated work.
Do not use `n.d.` or `n. pag.` for missing information unless the instructor requests it.
Facts recovered from reliable external evidence can be bracketed, with provenance retained.

Alphabetize entries by their leading element, using titles when entries have no author; ignore
initial A, An, or The when sorting titles. Use hanging indents and double spacing in the rendered list.
For repeated-author substitutions or specialized sorting, consult the current handbook rather than
merging authors who only share a first author or reordering coauthors.

## Starter patterns

Brackets below describe slots, never facts to invent. Omit absent optional slots and repair punctuation.

| Source | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Whole book | Author. *Book Title*. Edition if stated, Publisher, Year. |
| Chapter in edited collection | Author. “Chapter Title.” *Book Title*, edited by Editor, Publisher, Year, pp. range. |
| Online journal article | Author. “Article Title.” *Journal*, vol. x, no. y, Date, pp. range. *Database if consulted*, DOI/permalink/URL. |
| Web page | Author if identified. “Page Title.” *Site*, distinct publisher if relevant, page date if supplied, URL. Accessed date when useful. |
| Online video | Creator if clear. “Displayed Video Title.” *Platform*, uploaded by Account when useful, Date, URL. |
| Personal interview | Interviewee. Interview. Conducted by Interviewer, Date. |

An unpublished interview can instead be documented with a global note when appropriate. A private
recording does not require a fictitious public URL or timestamps. A publicly available recording is
cited as the actual audio/video version. See the interview sources in the source register.

## Generative AI

As checked 2026-10-25, use MLA's **13 August 2025** revised guidance, not the superseded 2023 examples:
omit an AI author; describe the generated material in the title slot, put the tool in the container
slot, record the specific verified model/version, company, and generation date, and prefer a stable
share link; use the tool's general URL if no stable link feature exists. Never invent a model name,
conversation link, or human author. Cite the original sources directly after reading them, rather
than laundering an AI summary's references. Explain substantive AI involvement separately as the
assignment requires. Citation mechanics grant no permission to use AI and no permission to publish
private chats; do not create a public link without authorization.
